Before starting Phase 1, I want a critical review of the current research plan.

Do NOT modify the code yet.

I want you to act as a skeptical research reviewer rather than as the project implementer.

Review the current PitchState research question, hypothesis, proposed contribution, baselines B0/B1/P1, evaluation metrics, and dataset strategy.

Specifically investigate:

1. NOVELTY
   Determine whether the proposed contribution is actually novel.

Search the relevant academic literature and existing football-analysis systems for work involving:

* broadcast football pitch calibration
* SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction
* temporal homography estimation
* player tracking and tracklet association
* team shape reconstruction
* confidence-aware / selective prediction
* uncertainty-aware sports analytics
* pitch-space player positioning
* tactical shape estimation from broadcast video

Identify papers, benchmarks, open-source implementations, or existing systems that are substantially similar.

Do not claim novelty merely because our implementation combines existing components.

Clearly distinguish:

* genuinely novel contribution
* engineering contribution
* reproduction/integration
* potentially novel combination that requires experimental validation

2. RESEARCH QUESTION

Critically evaluate whether the current research question is:

* sufficiently novel
* experimentally testable
* narrow enough
* technically feasible
* meaningful to football analytics
* publishable as a research-engineering project

If it is weak, propose a better research question.

Do not change it simply to make it sound more impressive.

3. HYPOTHESIS

Check whether the current hypothesis actually follows from the proposed methods.

Identify hidden assumptions.

For example:

* Does temporal calibration necessarily improve pitch-space shape accuracy?
* Can appearance-assisted tracklet association meaningfully improve shape metrics?
* Does abstention improve reliability without simply hiding failures?
* How should selective performance be compared fairly against a system that always outputs a prediction?

If necessary, rewrite the hypothesis into a falsifiable form.

4. EVALUATION VALIDITY

This is extremely important.

Audit every proposed metric.

For each metric explain:

* What it measures
* Why it is appropriate
* What ground truth is required
* Whether the available datasets actually provide that ground truth
* Potential confounders
* Whether optimizing it could produce misleading conclusions

Pay particular attention to:

* team centroid
* width
* depth
* pairwise spacing
* compactness
* temporal jitter
* frame-weighted team purity
* risk-coverage
* abstention rate

Determine whether these metrics are mathematically well-defined and robust to missing players.

5. AVOIDING THE "ABSTENTION TRICK"

Design a rigorous evaluation protocol that prevents the system from appearing better simply by abstaining on difficult frames.

Compare systems at:

* equal coverage
* equal confidence thresholds where appropriate
* full coverage
* predefined coverage levels

Define what constitutes useful abstention versus excessive abstention.

The proposed system must not be allowed to improve its score simply by reporting fewer frames.

6. DATASET AUDIT

Verify the actual contents, annotations, splits, and evaluation protocols of the proposed datasets.

Especially verify:

* SoccerNet-GSR
* SoccerNet Tracking
* SoccerNet Camera Calibration

Do not rely on memory or assumptions.

For every dataset state exactly what can and cannot be evaluated.

Also identify whether we need a custom annotated evaluation subset.

7. BASELINE FAIRNESS

Audit B0, B1, and P1.

Make sure the comparison isolates the actual research contribution.

For example, if P1 uses a better detector, better tracker, and better calibration simultaneously, we cannot attribute improvements to temporal calibration or confidence handling.

Design controlled ablations such as:

B1
B1 + temporal calibration
B1 + improved association
B1 + confidence gating
B1 + temporal calibration + association
B1 + all components

8. FAILURE ANALYSIS

Define a formal failure taxonomy:

* detector failure
* identity failure
* team-classification failure
* calibration failure
* camera-cut failure
* replay failure
* occlusion
* missing players
* false players
* footpoint error
* homography error
* temporal drift

Explain how each failure should be detected and how it propagates into the final tactical metrics.

9. RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION

At the end, give me three possible contribution levels:

A. Conservative
A strong engineering/reproducibility contribution.

B. Research-oriented
A measurable methodological contribution.

C. Strongest defensible version
The most ambitious contribution that can still be scientifically defended using the available data.

For each one, explain exactly what evidence would be required to support the claim.

10. FINAL DECISION

Conclude with one of:

KEEP
MODIFY
RETHINK

If the answer is MODIFY or RETHINK, provide the revised research question and explain why.

Do not write implementation code.

Do not start Phase 1.

The objective of this review is to make sure we are not building a technically impressive system around a weak or already-solved research question.
